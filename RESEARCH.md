## Family and shop
- Pascal Difazio came to England from central Italy, much of the way on foot. He landed at Falmouth and settled in Frome. [CM]
- In 1914 he opened a bicycle sales and repair shop at 25 Catherine Street, Frome. His son John was born the same year. [CM] Grace's Guide gives Jack's dates as c1914–c2012. [GG]
- New motorcycles came by train from the Midlands factories to Frome station. The shop sold BSA, Triumph and Brough. [CM]
- Jack recalled George Brough and T. E. Lawrence visiting the shop in the mid-1920s. ⚠ This is Jack's recollection as reported in [CM]. The editor notes it may have been Brough or Brough Superior.
- Post-war agencies: BSA, Triumph, Ariel, Velocette, Norton, Francis-Barnett and James. Young Richard collected new bikes from the station on the "works" BSA with a float sidecar. [CM]
- Japanese agencies: Honda in 1962 (supplied via Len Meredith's in Bristol), Suzuki in 1963, Yamaha in 1965. [CM]
- In the 1980s the shop turned to BMW twins and became one of the UK's leading independent BMW specialists. [CM]
- Address in 2007: Difazio Motorcycles, 25 Catherine Street, Frome BA11 1DB, tel. 01373 462913, contact Richard. [DW]

## Jack (John) Difazio
- He began grass-track racing just after his 16th birthday on a BSA Blue Star, then moved to a Rudge Ulster, which he raced both before and after the war. [CM]
- ⚠ Sources disagree on the nickname: the history page says he became "Jack" in his grass-track days, the hub article says when he started road racing. [CM]
- Before 1947 he worked for Pinks in London. He returned in 1947 to take over the business. [CM]
- His race-bike modifications included a twin front brake and home-made leading-axle forks. [CM]
- 1956: the first hub-centre machine, a BSA Gold Flash sidecar outfit. It used a Reliant-type single-sided swinging arm, a car shock absorber, and a Ford Prefect brake drum. Jack raced it with passenger Brian Sherwood. [CM]
- When Richard started racing in 1959, Jack retired from racing. The Body brothers bought the outfit, and Henry Body still had the chassis 47 years later. [CM]
- The solo design came to him on a Sunday drive near Stonehenge with his wife Sylvia, sketched on the back of an envelope. Disc brakes, pioneered on bikes by the Rickman brothers, solved the problem of the drum and king-pin "wanting to share the same space". [CM]
- Lockheed told him "a single disc is the ultimate for any motorcycle". He fitted two anyway. [CM]
- The first hub was cast iron. Later hubs were turned from solid aluminium billet. [CM]
- At 93 he was still driving himself to John Ransome's workshop to work on fitting a hub to a Honda Fireblade. [CM]
- He reached at least 90: an owner reports attending his 90th birthday party. He died in 2012, reported in Classic Bike. [LF]

## Hub-centre steering
- Patent GB1274441: filed 2 July 1968, applicant John Difazio. The record shows 1 July 1969 and 17 May 1972 in different fields. The design puts an inner hub on a king-pin inside an outer hub carrying twin discs, with suspension arms and push-pull radius arms. [PAT]
- No other Difazio patents were found.
- First solo rolling chassis: a racer with an ex-Mike Hailwood AJS 7R engine and a six-speed gearbox, raced by Richard at Thruxton and Cadwell Park. [CM] Richard confirms the first solo was "entirely of our manufacture… using the engine and transmission and rear wheel of an AJS 7R". He is pictured on it at Thruxton in 1968. [TCM]
- The second racer had a 500cc Suzuki engine. At Westonzoyland airfield it wouldn't run straight until the trail was set to 22°. Suzuki supplied "Daytona spec" tuning tips. [CM]
- The first road bike was Triumph Trident-powered and seen in Frome in autumn 1969. [CM]
- Suzuki 500 EYB 999J was road-tested by several journalists and later sold to a customer in the USA. [CM]
- Features included GRP bodywork, car-type switchgear, fairing luggage space, a fuel gauge and enclosed chains. [CM][FT]
- University of Manchester, 500-mile test: "up to 80mph the handlebars were almost superfluous… One could almost indulge in… eating a sandwich in the 'cockpit'." "40lbs placed anywhere on the machine produced no detectable effect on stability." Conclusion: "The Difazio is the best overall machine currently available for high speed touring." [CM]
- Output: about 50 machines between 1968 and 1983, built by Jack and a team of three. About half survive. There was interest from both BMW and Norton, but no commercial take-up. [FT]
- The team: Pat Hooper, Trevor Burridge and Tim Tucker. [CM]
- The workshops were in a former abattoir behind the shop. [FT]
- Only two complete Difazio motorcycles were built, one BSA triple-engined and one Ducati-engined. [BD]

## Richard Difazio
- He joined the business on leaving school in 1957. He spent two seasons as his father's grass-track passenger, then started road racing in 1959 on a Gold Star. Later he raced Manx Nortons prepared by Jack, and in the early 1970s a Difazio hub-steered Suzuki twin. That made 13 seasons with many National podiums. [CM]
- Thruxton, 1968: he raced the AJS 7R hub-centre special. [TCM]
- He built five lightweight BMW R65-based "Difazio Special" roadsters. The fifth, built for himself in 2007/8, is a 750cc ceramic-bore monolever machine weighing about 215 kg fuelled, and returns 50+ mpg. Earlier ones were 854cc. It was ridden in the Exeter Trial in 2010. [CSB]
- Richard says Difazio "built the first Mead and Tomkinson 'Nessie' for them". [TCM] Bonhams says Difazio supplied M&T's first two chassis. [BN]

## Machines (owners/years as sourced)
- **1975 Difazio Ducati 750GTE** (frame HS/DUC/129). It has a rare electric-start roundcase engine, one of only 400 built. It was renovated in 1991 and won the Bob Currey Best Engineering prize at Stafford and third place at World Ducati Week, Misano. Bonhams sold it on 20 Oct 2019 for £12,650. [BD]
- **BSA Rocket 3, 1971 (Kevin Charles)**. Owned since 1975. He sold it in 1977 as a deposit on his first house and bought it back 18 months later. Originally white and Triumph-badged, it was repainted BSA racing red after a van reversed into it. It is the only Rocket 3 hub-steerer; there were four Tridents. [CM]
- **Triumph Hurricane-engined Difazio (Kevin Charles)**. One of three built in spring 1976. It won the Kay Engineering Trophy at the Stafford Classic Bike Show in 2012. [FT]
- **Honda Gold Wing (Dave Ayesthorpe)**. Bought in 1977 and hub-converted in 1985. A second hub was fitted in 1987 after a crash. It has done 100,000+ miles. It is one of two hub-steered Gold Wings. [CM]
- **BMW R90S (John May)**. Originally fitted with a Falcon touring fairing, which took three hours to remove. [CM]
- **Suzuki GT550 (Andy Westlake)**. Built in 1973 from a written-off GT550K. First owned by RAF fitter Roger Margetts. Featured in *Bike* in 1974. Bought by phone at auction in 2005 for £1,500 with 17,000 miles. [CM]
- **Norton Commando (ex-press fleet)**. Registered Nov 1973 to Andover Norton as a press bike, and road-tested in *Motor Cycle Mechanics* in Feb 1974. Mike Jackson had Jack convert it, and the owner says the Norton board only learned of it later. Mike Jackson rode it in a Pyrenees event in the mid-1970s. It was restored around 2005–07, with frame and wheels by John Ransome. Pictured in Roy Bacon's *Norton Twins*, p.151. ⚠ Source is the owner's forum post. [AN]
- **Mead & Tomkinson "Nessie"**. A Laverda 1000 3C endurance racer on a Difazio hub-centre chassis from 1975. M&T switched to Kawasaki power in 1977. The best result was 10th at the Barcelona 24 Hours in 1976 (Norman White and Neil Tuxworth). [BN][MTW] ⚠ The name "Nessie" is applied variously to the Laverda and Kawasaki machines. [MTC]
- **Recreations by John Ransome** (ex-Difazio employee who acquired the moulds and spares in the early 1990s): a 500cc Suzuki twin racer and the Laverda "Nessie". [CM]
- **BMW R75/5 or /6 barn find**. A Difazio BMW with a huge fairing, found by the oldracer.co.uk blogger. [OR]
- **Suzuki T500/GT550 hub-steerers**, photographed in a Flickr album by teamheronsuzuki (licence not stated). [Flickr]

## Surprising facts (round 3)
- [WPH] Wikipedia, Hub-center steering: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hub-center_steering
  - The concept goes back to a 1910 James and the 1920 Ner-a-Car. It "enjoyed an aftermarket vogue in the 1970s through the work of Jack Difazio".
  - Tomkinson's efforts "encouraged the Elf racing team" in the 1980s.
  - Michael Tryphonos built a prototype "based on the Difazio system" that reached 11th in the Senior TT.
  - The Yamaha GTS1000 was based on James Parker's RADD design.
- [ALTO] Alto Racing, "Hub centre - a brief history": http://www.altoracing.co.uk/super-bike-project/the-bike/
  - Royce Creasey "worked with Difazio for his own first project".
  - The Bimota Tesi 1D came from the Elf-era line, not Difazio's. Vyrus (Ascanio Rodorigo, a former Bimota technician) built the Tesi 2D.
- [FFW] FF Web (bikeweb.com): "Creasey-Difazio CX500 Flying Banana (1981)": https://www.bikeweb.com/image/tid/36 · Royce Creasey's own comment: https://www.bikeweb.com/node/3750.html
  - At the 1981 Isle of Man TT, Royce put in "a 33 minute open-roads lap" and later crashed at Creg ny Baa (he wrote "Creg na Baa").
- [CMM] cybermotorcycle, Difazio marque page: https://cybermotorcycle.com/marques/british/difazio.htm
  - *The Motor Cycle*, 26 June 1968: the AJS 7R special "made quite a stir" at Thruxton.
  - Tim Tucker (2014), describing himself as the last surviving member of the original team: bikes were built for the USA, a Suzuki 750 went to Canada (1974), and BMW OXB 75P plus a sister Suzuki 500 went to Australia in 1976. One owner's wife returned her Difazio Suzuki "as she couldn't cope with it".
  - The later Australian owner of the BMW called it "the best handling boxer twin I ever owned".
  - A Difazio-Suzuki 350 was built for a customer using a 325cc racing engine.
- ⚠ Memory-only, kept but labelled on the site: the Brough / T. E. Lawrence visit (Jack's recollection, [CM]).

## Tim Tucker (team member; the user's dad)
- He was part of Jack's usual team, with Pat Hooper and Trevor Burridge. [CM] https://www.cybermotorcycle.com/archives/difazio-history.htm
- The team (Jack, Trevor Burridge, Tim Tucker, Pat Hooper) made the frames, turned the hubs, laid the fibreglass and did the assembly, "very much a cottage industry". [CM/DW]
- 2008 email to cybermotorcycle, signed "Tim": he built BMW UK reg OXB 75P and its sister Suzuki 500 for a couple living in Adelaide in 1976, who took both bikes to Australia. He asked for photos "to add to the records". https://cybermotorcycle.com/marques/british/difazio.htm
- 23 Jan 2014 email from "Tim Tucker": "The Difazio Hub Steered BMW motorcycle which I built back in 1975" was bought by Robert Jameison and taken to Australia. "It's the only bike of ours in Australia so it is important to us to locate it." He put an alert out to bike clubs. [same URL]
- Same email:
  - He spoke to an owner of "a similar bike that I built in the USA".
  - He located "a Difazio Suzuki 750 which I built in 1974", shipped to Canada and now in "Birmingham AB USA" (as written).
  - "We also shipped back a Suzuki 500 Falcon from the USA for restoration."
  - "We now have quite a collection back in the UK most of which are still ridden regularly."
  - "I am the only one around from the original Team that built these bikes but have great enjoyment helping towards restoration and attending shows."
  - Signed "Tim, Difazio Motorcycle Interest Group UK". [same URL]
- ⚠ Discrepancy: the 2008 email dates the Australia pair to 1976, the 2014 email says the BMW was built in 1975. The site uses Tim's 2014 wording.
- ⚠ The Access Norton Commando owner who signs "Tim" is a different person: an ex-Army owner in Andover whose father bought the bike in 1977.
- Gaps: no dates for Tim's employment, no photos, no press quotes, and no anecdotes with Jack, Richard, Pat or Trevor found online. Frome Times and Classic Bike Hub don't name him.
